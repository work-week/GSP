# 2026-10-19 — Direct competitors: New Jersey, East Coast, United States

**Method:** web searches for B2B international forwarders by region and specialty (project cargo, heavy lift, NVOCC, Korea/Japan lanes, Chicago), directory lists (Inven, Breakbulk exhibitors), then reading the sites of the closest matches. Korean-American forwarders were searched in English first (no useful results), then in Korean the same evening (see [that section](#korean-american-forwarders)). Claims are as the sites state them; credentials not independently verified. Earlier design references are in [competitors](2026-10-19-competitors.md).

**Link key:** a link marked *(read)* was opened and read for this research. *(from directory)* means the address comes from a directory listing and the site itself was not opened.

## How "direct competitor" is defined here

A business that would compete with GSP for the same deal: a B2B importer or exporter (or an EPC contractor) choosing a forwarder for ocean, air, customs or project cargo, from the NY/NJ or Chicago area.

### Korean-American forwarders

**How this was found (and what is and is not known).** English searches for Korean-American forwarders around Fort Lee returned no forwarder websites, only importer records and directory stubs. Searching **in Korean** found them: a Korean-American business directory for New York and New Jersey lists **128 businesses** in its trade and freight category (무역/통운), separate from courier (40) and moving (134) categories. Only the first page of 10 was read.

- **Directory:** [Korea Portal NY/NJ: 무역/통운 (trade and freight)](https://ny.koreaportal.com/yp/yp_list.php?mcode=ent&scode=ent_02) *(read, page 1 of 13)*
- From page 1, the ones that look like freight forwarders or customs brokers:
  - **Far East Forwarding, Inc.** (김의만 관세사), Jamaica, NY near JFK: customs broker, 37 years. No website listed.
  - **Phoenix Cargo** (피닉스해운항공), North Bergen, NJ: sea and air cargo, by name. No website listed.
  - **Canaan Int'l Freight, Inc.** (가나안국제운송), Jamaica, NY. No website listed.
  - **Corea Int'l Logistics** (대한국제물류), Flushing, NY. No website listed.
  - **STX Pan Ocean (America)**, Rutherford, NJ: U.S. arm of a Korean shipping line, [panocean.com](https://www.panocean.com/) *(from directory)*.
- Mostly consumer (returning-to-Korea moves, parcels), so less direct:
  - **Broad Hanjin / Hanjin Shipping, Inc.** (한진택배), Palisades Park, NJ: [broadhanjin.com](https://broadhanjin.com/) *(read)*. Korean-only site, parcels and moves to Korea.
  - **GSI Express Logistics**, South Hackensack, NJ, since 2013: [gsiexpress.com](https://www.gsiexpress.com/about_company.php) *(read)*. U.S.-to-Korea air express, mostly for online sellers.
  - **CJ USA**, Jamaica, NY: [cjusa.net](http://cjusa.net/) *(from directory)*. Parcels to Korea.

**What this tells us:** the Korean-American freight community is real and sizable, but mostly offline: many listings have no website at all, and those that do are Korean-only and dated. This supports, but does not prove, the idea that these firms compete through Korean-language channels and referrals. It also means a GSP site with good **Korean-language pages** would face almost no online competition in that community.

Also seen in Korean search results, not yet opened: [Hana Logistics](https://hanalogistics.com/) and [TPL Korea](http://www.tplkorea.net/). Large Korean logistics groups with U.S. operations (for example Hanjin, CJ Logistics, LX Pantos) compete on the Korea lane at much larger scale; not researched yet.

## What this means for GSP

1. **The two to beat are IFT and Atlantic Pacific Lines.** Both are New Jersey NVOCCs that market project cargo and heavy lift, show credentials and publish content. They are the benchmark for the new site.
2. **GSP's honest edge against them:** founder-led personal service (the one testimonial is about Jason by name), Korean and Japanese language, and two offices (NJ and Chicago). Neither competitor claims those.
3. **Credentials are table stakes** against these two. If GSP holds an FMC license, C-TPAT or IATA, they must be on the site.
4. **Make content readable without JavaScript.** IFT's content seems invisible to readers that do not run scripts, which can include some AI crawlers. Webflow and a server-rendered SvelteKit site avoid this.
5. **The online bar for project cargo is low.** Real case studies with weights, routes and photos would beat most ranking pages.
6. **Korean-language pages** would face almost no online competition among Korean-American shippers.
7. **Partners, not just rivals:** Harbor Freight Transport, heavy-haul brokers, crating and crane companies.
