**Method:** web searches for B2B international forwarders by region and specialty (project cargo, heavy lift, NVOCC, Korea/Japan lanes, Chicago), directory lists (Inven, Breakbulk exhibitors), then reading the sites of the closest matches. Claims are as the sites state them; credentials not independently verified. Earlier design references are in [competitors](2026-10-19-competitors.md).

## How "direct competitor" is defined here

A business that would compete with GSP for the same deal: a B2B importer or exporter (or an EPC contractor) choosing a forwarder for ocean, air, customs or project cargo, from the NY/NJ or Chicago area.

## Ring 2: East Coast

- **Harbor Freight Transport Corp.** (Port Newark, NJ, since 1947; also Savannah, GA): bonded warehousing, trucking, out-of-gauge handling and heavy-lift cranes **inside the port**. More a **partner** than a competitor: GSP could use them for OOG cargo moving through Newark.
- **Book Your Cargo / gobookfreight.com:** a heavy-haul trucking broker (FMCSA licensed) for project cargo moving inland from ports, including a case study of a 223,000 lb evaporator. Sells **to** forwarders and EPC firms, so also a partner. Its project cargo page is a good content model: "what to send us" checklist, FAQ, and a real case study.
- The search did not surface a mid-size East Coast project cargo forwarder with a strong web presence beyond IFT and APL in New Jersey. **The East Coast niche is thin online**, which is an opportunity.

## Ring 3: United States

- **Project cargo pages that rank** (for example Progressive Cargo, USI Ship, Spatial Global) share the same weaknesses: no licenses shown, no named clients, no real case studies, generic FAQ, typos. Ranking with thin content means the bar is low.
- **National "best forwarder" lists** are written by brokers and forwarders ranking themselves or the giants (Kuehne + Nagel, DSV, DHL, C.H. Robinson, Expeditors, Flexport). AI answer engines cite these lists. GSP will not top them; it can win the **niche** questions (project cargo from NJ/Chicago, Korea and Japan lanes) where no list exists yet.
- **Chicago:** results are dominated by the giants and by ExFreight's self-ranking list. No small Chicago project cargo forwarder stood out. Barnhart Crane (Chicago) appears for heavy lift, as a crane and rigging provider, again a potential partner.

## What this means for GSP

1. **The two to beat are IFT and Atlantic Pacific Lines.** Both are New Jersey NVOCCs that market project cargo and heavy lift, show credentials and publish content. They are the benchmark for the new site.
2. **GSP's honest edge against them:** founder-led personal service (the one testimonial is about Jason by name), Korean and Japanese language, and two offices (NJ and Chicago). Neither competitor claims those.
3. **Credentials are table stakes** against these two. If GSP holds an FMC license, C-TPAT or IATA, they must be on the site.
4. **Make content readable without JavaScript.** IFT's content seems invisible to readers that do not run scripts, which can include some AI crawlers. Webflow and a server-rendered SvelteKit site avoid this.
5. **The online bar for project cargo is low.** Real case studies with weights, routes and photos would beat most ranking pages.
6. **Partners, not just rivals:** Harbor Freight Transport, heavy-haul brokers, crating and crane companies.
