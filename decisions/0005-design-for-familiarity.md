# 0005 — Design for familiarity, shaped by the ideal client

**Date:** 2026-10-19
**Decided by:** Krischan
**Status:** accepted

## Context

The strongest competitor site (Atlantic Pacific Lines) is modern and polished. Polished is not the same as right for GSP's buyers. The site should be shaped by who GSP actually serves, and who it wants to serve, before any visual style is chosen.

## Decision

1. **Define the ideal client profile (ICP) first.** Ask the GSP team, and check it against real data (see "Evidence" below). The site is tailored to that profile.
2. **Follow Jakob's law** (from Laws of UX): people spend most of their time on other sites, so they expect ours to work the same way. Utilitarian by choice and by design:
   - Navigation visible as plain words. No important menus hidden behind small icon buttons on desktop, no mega menus inside mega menus.
   - The things buyers come for are always one click away: services, request a quote, phone, email, offices.
   - Familiar patterns over clever ones: standard page layouts, readable type sizes, clear labels, tables where data is tabular.
   - Beauty supports clarity; it never costs usability.
3. **Order of audiences** follows the ICP once known (for example, primary buyers first, then secondary audiences).

## Why

The goal is to win not through ad spend but because "the site just works and feels familiar", reducing the visitor's cognitive load. A beautiful site that misses its market fails.

## Evidence for the ICP

- **The GSP team:** who their best customers are (industry, company size, the person who books freight, countries).
- **Their own records:** customer lists, invoices or quotes, grouped by industry, country and role.
- **Search Console:** search queries, countries, devices and pages. It does **not** show age or gender.
- **GA4:** age, gender and interests only with Google signals enabled and enough traffic; at GSP's current traffic it will likely show little.
