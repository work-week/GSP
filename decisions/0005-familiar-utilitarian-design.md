# 0005 — Familiar, utilitarian design for GSP's actual buyers

**Date:** 2026-10-19
**Decided by:** Krischan
**Status:** accepted

## Context

The strongest competitor sites (for example Atlantic Pacific Lines) look modern and polished. Polish is not the goal if it makes the site harder for GSP's buyers to use.

## Decision

Design for the ideal customer profile (ICP), following Jakob's law: users spend most of their time on other sites and expect this one to work the same way. The site is utilitarian by choice:

- Plain, visible navigation with words, not icons. No hamburger menu on desktop, no nested mega menus.
- Phone number, quote button and office details always easy to find.
- Readable type, clear contrast, few clicks to a quote.
- Familiar page patterns over novel ones.

The aim is to compete on "the site just works and feels familiar", reducing the visitor's effort, rather than on ad spend or visual flash.

## How it is applied

1. Confirm the ICP with Jason (see [next meeting](../project/next-meeting.md)).
2. Check who actually arrives, once data exists. See [What the data can and cannot tell us](#what-the-data-can-and-cannot-tell-us).
3. Borrow competitors' substance (credentials, content, quote flow), not their visual style.

## What the data can and cannot tell us

- **Search Console** shows search queries, countries, devices and landing pages. It does not show age or gender.
- **GA4** can show age, gender and interests only with Google signals on, and hides them below privacy thresholds. GSP's traffic is too low for this to show much at first.
- For a B2B buyer, **firmographics** matter more than demographics: industry, company size, the buyer's role, country, language. These come mostly from Jason and from quote form fields, not from analytics.
